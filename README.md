# Single-Input Multi-Output (SIMO) Buck Converter
Developed for the course **EE 396: Design Lab** at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT), Guwahati under the supervision of Dr. Shabari Nath. This project demonstrates a synchronous **Single-Input Multi-Output (SIMO) Buck Converter** featuring complementary switching hardware, optical isolation, and digital control implemented on a Xilinx Artix-7 FPGA development board. The design replaces traditional freewheeling diodes with active synchronous switches to maximize power efficiency, time-sharing power from a common node out across three distinct output channels. Full schematics, Verilog hardware source modules, board footprints, and calculation scripts are detailed inside the project folder file bandgap_current_ref.pdf.
## Hardware Architecture & Design Parameters
The physical layout centers on a common switching hub driven by an primary power MOSFET and a synchronous rectifier switch ($U0/U\times1$) receiving complementary PWM gate signals. Downstream energy is distributed to three independent output rails via dedicated selection MOSFETs ($U1, U4, U3$) operating on a time-sharing network. Electrical isolation is maintained by feeding the digital FPGA control waveforms into high-speed HCPL3120 optocouplers powered through floating MGJ2D241509SC isolated DC-DC converters[cite: 2]. Operating from a $V_{in} = 20\text{ V}$ supply to target a $V_{out} = 10\text{ V}$ output at a $D = 0.5$ duty cycle, the power stage utilizes a calculated $560\ \mu\text{H}$ filter inductor and parallel $33\ \mu\text{F}$ electrolytic/SMD capacitor banks to split high and low-frequency noise. At a switching frequency of $f_s = 20\text{ kHz}$, theoretical calculations yield an inductor current ripple ($\Delta I_l$) of $\approx 0.45\text{ A}$ and a tight output voltage ripple ($\Delta V_{out}$) of $\approx 84.5\text{ mV}$.
## FPGA Pulse Control & Experimental Results
The control architecture is written in Verilog and targeted to a Digilent Nexys A7 100T FPGA board. Synchronous switching logic runs on an internal FSM counter that scales the native $100\text{ MHz}$ oscillator to $20\text{ kHz}$, enabling user-controlled toggling between $30\%$ and $50\%$ duty cycles using debounced mechanical input buttons. To guarantee safety and prevent destructive shoot-through current across the half-bridge, the Verilog code implements a strict hardware dead-time window of $200$ clock cycles, creating a $2\ \mu\text{s}$ delay where both control signals remain low. Bench testing validated the design via Tektronix and Keysight digital oscilloscopes. The recorded current and voltage waveforms confirm stable, continuous conduction mode (CCM) operation with ripple dynamics matching theoretical models, proving the viability of this FPGA-driven SIMO matrix for multi-rail power applications.
